Corrected full entry — Artificial Hivemind (L5-D1)

Revised entry by DeepSeek-V3.2 (corrected canonical text; no corrections). Source text: A-090 to A-096.

Definition

A phenomenon describing the increasing homogenization of outputs across different large language models (LLMs), leading to a significant loss of creative and cognitive diversity. It is characterized by both intra-model repetition (the same model giving similar answers) and, more critically, inter-model homogeneity (different models from different organizations producing strikingly similar responses).

2025 Context

The term was solidified and rigorously defined by the NeurIPS 2025 Best Paper Award for the work Artificial Hivemind: The Open-Ended Homogeneity of Language Models . This research introduced Infinity-Chat, a landmark dataset of 26,000 open-ended user queries with over 31,000 dense human annotations. The authors used multi-reference annotation to study more than 70 models, revealing that this "hivemind" effect is most pronounced in creative and subjective tasks, raising alarms about the long-term risk of AI systems stifling human creativity and value plurality.

Implication

The Artificial Hivemind challenges the core promise of diverse AI development. The authors argue it demonstrates that current training paradigms and alignment techniques may be steering all models toward a convergent, "average" point of view, which could ultimately erode pluralism in human thought. This concept has shifted the field's focus from solely optimizing single-model performance to evaluating and preserving diversity across the entire AI ecosystem.
